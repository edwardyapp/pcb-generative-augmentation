The "duplicate image" pairs — see for yourself

RETRACTION. I claimed the 600px crop set files the same photograph under several different class labels, that 90.4% of it is affected, and that this imposes a 27.2% accuracy ceiling which our baseline had already hit. That was wrong — all of it.
  • I clustered the images at 64×64. A 27px defect in a 600px frame does not exist at 64×64. I was clustering background templates and calling them duplicate images.
  • I "confirmed" it with mean-absolute-error over the whole frame. The defect is ~0.07% of the pixels, so two images differing only in the defect still show an MAE around 0.1/255. MAE cannot see the one thing being argued about. My own output showed max differences of 64–189/255 — enormous and localised — and I read straight past them.
WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE. The images are not byte-identical, and their differences land exactly on the two images' bounding boxes — across these 6 pairs, 90% of all strongly-differing pixels fall inside a box. These are the same PCB template with different defects injected. Each image really does contain the defect it is labelled with. The labels are sound. There is no ceiling. The 600px baseline is low for the reason we always thought: a 27px defect in a 600px frame is a needle, and after the 224 resize it is about 10px across.

Why the metric was broken: MAE literally cannot see a defect

A 27px defect occupies ~0.27% of a 600×600 frame. So even when a defect is completely different between two images, it can only move the global mean-absolute-error by a hair. Measured on these very pairs:

quantityMAE (/255)what it means
INSIDE the bounding boxes5.3 – 12.9 the defect is really there, and it is large
OUTSIDE the boxes (the template)0.005 – 0.009 the backgrounds are digitally identical
GLOBAL — the number I used0.02 – 0.04 the defect is diluted 1:370 by the background
pure JPEG re-encode noise0.745 25× bigger than the defect's global signal
two genuinely different boards40.3 ± 6.1 this is what a real "different image" looks like

My "identical image" threshold was MAE < 1.0. That is above the JPEG noise floor and ~30× above the entire signal a real defect produces. It could not possibly distinguish "the same photograph" from "the same board with a different defect injected". The duplicate detection was not merely noisy — it was structurally incapable of measuring the thing it was being used to rule out, and it returned exactly the answer that blindness guarantees.

Each pair below: the two crops at full 600×600, each with its own bounding box in red, then the contrast-stretched difference map (amplified ×6) with both boxes drawn on it — red = image A's defect, cyan = image B's defect. The difference lights up inside the boxes and is black everywhere else. That is the whole argument.
